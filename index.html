Discuss a mandate

I walked into a life sciences company where revenue growth had stalled after four consecutive years of double digits. Everyone said it was a technology problem. It wasn't. The dev teams had deskilled, lost confidence, and couldn't deploy properly. I promised the CMO monthly deliveries, didn't say what; by the end we were shipping weekly. Devs started seeing their work connect to business outcomes for the first time. Growth came back. The tech stack barely changed.

At a FTSE 250 I brought five brands together under a single delivery function for the first time. Nobody could see what anyone else was doing. Then the Ukraine invasion happened and our entire digital operation depended on one Ukrainian partner. I set direction: people first, operations second. Stepped back and let the permanent team lead what needed their relationships. Sourced alternatives in parallel. People safe, systems secure, and a business with choices it didn't have before.

In government I wrote governance principles for agile delivery that are still published on GOV.UK twelve years later. Coached the team that replaced paper driving licence counterparts for 40 million people. Led a 150-person cross-supplier team delivering the digital service that went on to distribute £2.7 billion a year in levy funds.

The last two years: CEO of CyberSafeSchools, a SaaS cyber safety provider helping UK schools meet DfE and NCSC cyber security standards. Co-founded beò to measure what AI adoption actually does to workforces. Authored CHART, an AI governance framework aimed at the EU AI Act's human-oversight obligations. Appointed to the ICAEW Regulatory Board as a lay member. The through-line hasn't changed.

The pattern is always the same: everyone says it's a technology problem. Knowing when it is, and when it isn't, is the judgement I'm hired for.

AI governance, when the mandate needs it

When a mandate runs into AI governance, and more of them do, I bring more than an opinion. I authored CHART (Cognitive Health Assessment for Responsible Technology), a framework for evidencing the meaningful human oversight the EU AI Act requires. The framework is openly published; commercial engagements run through beò, the research company I co-founded, separately from my interim practice.
